How it works

One beat, step by step

1

Center sends BEAT b (only after the ACK for b−1).

2

Each follower finds its target for b in the stored plan (no plan: hold).

3

Release the cell you are in, then wait at the barrier.

4

Claim the target cell in the grid, then the barrier again.

5

The thread the barrier names sends the POS lines and ACK b.

6

Center audits collisions and speed, then sends BEAT b+1.

Why two barriers?

In one beat, follower A may move into the cell follower B is leaving.

All releases must happen before any claim, or A’s claim fails by accident.

A failed claim is a safety violation, never something to retry in a loop.

BIL 322 · Swarm Phase 1 lab

This is the heart of Tasks 3 and 5. The center sends BEAT b to every leader, and never sends b+1 before it has the ACK for b: that keeps everyone in lockstep. In the leader, the main thread publishes the beat; each follower wakes up, looks up its target in the stored plan, and holds its position if there is no plan. Then two phases separated by barriers. Phase 1: everyone releases the cell it is in; barrier. Phase 2: everyone claims its target cell; barrier. Why two? Followers move at the same time. If A wants the cell that B is leaving, A’s claim would fail if B had not released it yet. With a barrier in between, all releases happen before any claim. After the second barrier, pthread_barrier_wait returns PTHREAD_BARRIER_SERIAL_THREAD to exactly one thread; that thread sends the POS lines for all followers and then the ACK. A failed claim is a safety violation, never something to retry in a loop. Think about it: what happens if one follower returns early and skips a barrier? Everyone else waits forever.
